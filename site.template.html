Three Possible Outcomes

From the start, there are only three. There is no Path 4.

Path 1

Full Compliance

  • Each day is documented.
  • Each weigh-in is posted.
  • Each entry is logged.

Completion requires sustained documentation. Every missed requirement remains part of the record.

Path 2

A Requirement Is Missed

  • The 10 PM check runs by itself and enters the date and what was missing.
  • A corrective session follows — recorded, published beside the entry, and verified by the Accountability Partner.
  • The entry stays. Completing the correction closes it; nothing removes it.

Nobody has to decide this. It happens whether or not anyone is watching.

Path 3

Project Abandonment

  • If the project is abandoned, the record remains public.
  • The starting weight, the last recorded date, and the unfinished goal remain visible.

There is no unrecorded ending to this project.

How the Record Is Administered

This project is not self-directed. Once the agreement is counter-signed, the Accountability Partner administers the official record. I do not.

I built this project and this website; control of the official record sits with the Accountability Partner. Previous attempts had the same weakness: I set the rules, which meant I could also move them. An earlier agreement, TF060, failed — that record is here. Under this one, nothing binds until it is written into the agreement or logged as an amendment I have co-signed, and once it binds I do not get to renegotiate it on a bad day.

Real name. The record is attached to my legal name because anonymity would weaken it. In the past it was too easy to restart privately, hide a failure, or abandon the process without a lasting record. The point is not branding; it is consequence.

Uniform and collar. Every official recording is made in a plain black unitard from the same four angles, so records from different days are directly comparable and presentation cannot make a result look better or worse. The collar is different in kind: a plain steel or titanium neck ring, worn continuously, that I asked for as a standing physical reminder that I am under the agreement — on camera and off. It has no comparison purpose. It is the one thing I cannot take off when compliance becomes inconvenient. Its absence from an official recording is a Violation Event (§4.1).

Daily documentation. Once active, the packet is due every day by 10:00 PM Eastern: a four-angle inspection video, four photographs, the scale-synced weight, and the tracker update. Daily, because long gaps create room for avoidance. It is either completed or it is not.

Misses and corrections. The record system flags a missed requirement; the Accountability Partner reviews the evidence and any documented exception; the confirmed outcome is published to the Violation Log; a corrective session — corner time, 10 / 20 / 30 minutes by level, recorded in one take — becomes due within 72 hours and is published beside the entry. Entries are retained for the life of the record; the only exceptions are the documented safety/privacy takedown (§8.6) and logged factual corrections.

Progress and scope. Daily weigh-ins and documented thresholds at 320, 300, 275, 250, 225, and 200 pounds show whether the project is working. Weight fluctuation is progress information, never a violation; only missing or false documentation is. The project enforces documentation, not medicine — medical decisions are made with a physician and sit outside the record (§6.4, §9). Evening Supervision (§3.4) is a proposed requirement, not active, described on the Protocol page.

Voluntarily established and governed by the written agreement. The arrangement ends only by completion or its terms (§11, §13).

Statement — in his own words

Why It Is Public

This project is public on purpose.

Private promises were too easy for me to renegotiate, postpone, excuse, or quietly abandon. Public accountability removes that comfort. My real name, face, starting point, daily record, missed requirements, and final outcome are attached to the commitment I made, and that exposure is meant to be uncomfortable.

I know that coworkers, friends, acquaintances, strangers, and people I may meet years from now can find this record and see my weight, my photographs, my inspections, my violations, and whether I kept my word. I accepted that deliberately. Being recognized from it is not an accident of the design; it is part of the mechanism, because there is supposed to be a difference between telling myself privately that I will change and attaching my name to a commitment other people can verify.

I am free to stop. The project does not make withdrawal impossible. But I did not build an easy way to erase the commitment afterward: if I abandon it before the completion standard is met, the record is designed to say exactly that. Abandoned is not another word for completed. I chose a system in which quitting has an observable consequence, because without one quitting would always be the easiest option.

That does not mean anything goes. The public project does not authorize harassment, threats, stalking, interference with my employment, publication of private information, or intrusion into my life. The discomfort comes from being seen clearly and held to the standard I chose — not from surrendering basic safety or dignity.

Anyone may link to this record, quote it unaltered, or repost its public material in context; the Share page has what an outside party needs. If I complete this, nobody will be able to dismiss the result as another promise or another restart. The same retention that makes failure uncomfortable is what gives completion meaning.
